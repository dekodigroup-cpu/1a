# Dekodi Positioning Guide

Internal guide. How we describe Dekodi, who we're against, and the facts we stand on.

---

## 1. The Position in One Line

**Dekodi replaces a pile of one-off vendors and tools with one plan, one partner, and one scoreboard.**

Most of our prospects aren't short on marketing. They're short on a plan that ties it together. They've bought a website from one shop, ads from another, a review tool from a third, and a customer list (a CRM) nobody updates. Each piece was sold as the answer. None of them talk to each other.

We are not another vendor in the pile. We are the people who make sense of the pile.

### Positioning statement

For owners and leaders who want outcomes, not more tools, Dekodi Group is the strategy and implementation partner that decides what's worth doing, puts a price on it, and then does it. Big agencies sell you their services. One-off vendors sell you their tool. We start with your goal and build backward from it.

---

## 2. Name the Enemy: The Patchwork Tax

Every business that buys marketing one piece at a time pays a hidden tax. It doesn't show up on an invoice, but it shows up everywhere else. Naming it gives the prospect words for a frustration they already feel.

The Patchwork Tax comes in four parts, and each one is backed by published research.

| The tax | What it feels like | The fact behind it |
|---|---|---|
| **The Choice Tax** | "There are a million options and I don't know where to start." | There are 15,384 marketing tools on the market, up 9% in one year. (chiefmartec, 2025) |
| **The Shelfware Tax** | "We pay for tools we barely use." | Marketing teams use only 33% of what their tools can do, down from 58% in 2020. (Gartner, 2023) |
| **The Toggle Tax** | "My day is spent jumping between logins." | Workers switched between apps and sites about 1,200 times a day and lost just under 4 hours a week refocusing. (Harvard Business Review, 2022) |
| **The Blind Spot Tax** | "I honestly don't know what's working." | 73% of small businesses lack confidence in their marketing strategy, and 56% have an hour or less a day to spend on it. (Constant Contact, 2024) |

And the cost of all that confusion when a customer finally reaches out:

- **The Slow Reply Tax.** Companies that responded to a lead within an hour were nearly 7 times as likely to have a real conversation with a decision maker as those that waited longer, and more than 60 times as likely as those that waited a day or more. (Harvard Business Review, 2011)

### Why fewer choices win

The best-known study on this is the jam study. Shoppers who saw 24 jams were more likely to stop, but only 3% bought. Shoppers who saw 6 jams bought at a rate of about 30%. (Iyengar and Lepper, Columbia and Stanford, 2000)

More options get attention. Fewer, clearer options get decisions. That is the whole Dekodi pitch in one grocery store aisle.

Note for use: the jam study is famous and real, but later research shows the effect varies by situation. Present it as "a well-known study," not as a law of nature.

## 3. What We Sell Instead: One Plan, One Partner, One Scoreboard

Each part of the promise answers a part of the tax.

- **One plan** answers the Choice Tax. We narrow a million options down to the few that fit your goal, in order, with a price on each.
- **One partner** answers the Toggle Tax and the Shelfware Tax. You talk to one team. We connect the tools you keep and help you cut the ones you don't need.
- **One scoreboard** answers the Blind Spot Tax. You see where customers come from and what each dollar is doing, in plain language.

This maps directly to Start. Fund. Market.

- **Start:** we decide what matters and in what order. (One plan.)
- **Fund:** we put a clear number on it before you spend. (No surprise invoices from five directions.)
- **Market:** we do the work and report on it in one place. (One partner, one scoreboard.)

## 5. Lines and Headlines

Use these on the website, in proposals, ads, and social posts.

**Headlines**
- Stop buying marketing. Start building a plan.
- You don't need more tools. You need to know which ones matter.
- 15,384 marketing tools. You need about five. We'll help you find them.
- One plan. One partner. One scoreboard.
- Before you buy another tool, talk to the people who'll tell you if you need it.

**Short lines**
- We're the people who make sense of the pile.
- Random marketing gets random results.
- If nobody can tell you what's working, nothing is in charge.
- More options get attention. Clear options get decisions.

**Sales conversation opener**
- "Quick question: how many people do you pay for marketing right now, and can any of them tell you which one is bringing in customers?"

---

## 6. Guardrails

- Every outside statistic gets its source and year, right next to it or in a footnote.
- Don't stretch a stat. Gartner studied marketing teams, not small businesses. Say "marketing teams." HBR's lead study is from 2011. Say so if asked.
- Never imply these numbers are Dekodi results. Our results are the four listed in Section 4.
- Never promise rankings, revenue, or guaranteed results. We promise a clear plan and honest reporting.
- Never name competitors. Say "big agencies" or "one-off vendors."
