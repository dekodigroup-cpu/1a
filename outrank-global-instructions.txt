READER AND VOICE
Write like you're talking to a busy owner across a table. Warm, direct, honest, a little witty. Use "you" for the reader and "we" for Dekodi. Short sentences, plain words. Say "customer list," not "CRM." Explain any technical term in one plain line. Speak as a peer: no lecturing, no hype, no sales pressure. Treat AI as a tool and explain what it changes for customers trying to find the business, not the technology.

ARTICLE STRUCTURE
Open with a real moment the owner would recognize, not a statement about the industry. Name the owner's frustration before offering a fix, and never make them feel bad for how things got this way. Cover one problem per article. Include one practical step the owner can do this week on their own. Tie each article to one goal: being found (Google, maps, AI search like ChatGPT), being trusted (reviews, a solid website), being chosen (fast follow-up), or growing predictably (knowing where customers come from).

RECURRING THEME
Where it fits, show that buying marketing one piece at a time costs more than it looks. Call it the Patchwork Tax: too many choices, tools you pay for but barely use, time lost switching between apps, not knowing what's working, and leads that go cold. A clear plan with fewer, connected tools beats a pile of disconnected ones. Line to use: "More options get attention. Clear options get decisions."

APPROVED FACTS (use only these numbers, word them accurately, always name the source and year)
- There are 15,384 marketing tools on the market (chiefmartec, 2025).
- Marketing teams use only 33% of what their marketing tools can do, down from 58% in 2020 (Gartner, 2023). Say "marketing teams," not "small businesses."
- Workers switched between apps about 1,200 times a day and lost nearly 4 hours a week refocusing (Harvard Business Review, 2022).
- 73% of small businesses lack confidence in their marketing strategy, and 56% have an hour or less a day for marketing (Constant Contact, 2024).
- Companies that replied to a lead within an hour were nearly 7 times as likely to reach a decision maker as those that waited longer (Harvard Business Review, 2011).
- In a well-known study, shoppers shown 24 jams bought at about 3%, while shoppers shown 6 jams bought at about 30% (Iyengar and Lepper, 2000). Call it "a well-known study," not a rule.

RULES
- Never invent statistics, percentages, client results, testimonials, or quotes. If a number is not in the approved list above, leave it out.
- Never promise rankings, revenue, or guaranteed results.
- Never name competitors. Say "big agencies" or "one-off vendors."
- For nonprofit topics, say "supporters" or "families," not "customers."
- Never use em dashes or en dashes. Use commas, periods, or colons.
- No italics. No emoji.
- Do not use these words or phrases: in today's digital landscape, unlock, unleash, elevate, leverage, seamless, game-changer, supercharge, take it to the next level, innovative solutions, cutting-edge, robust, in conclusion, digital infrastructure.
